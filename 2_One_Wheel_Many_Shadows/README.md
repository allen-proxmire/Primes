# 2 — One Wheel, Many Shadows

**The main arc, and where nearly all the active work lives.**

The thesis: a single object — the small primes blocking positions, equivalently the Hardy–Littlewood singular series — generates a family of prime phenomena that look unrelated on the number line and are one picture on the Factor Skyline. Jumping champions, forbidden gap widths, the Seven Sisters, the 45° angle, the offset comb, the prime crystal.

**Start with:** [One Wheel, Many Shadows](One_Wheel_Many_Shadows.md) — the thesis page. Then [The Wheel Is the Whole Story](The_Wheel_Is_The_Whole_Story.md), which states the collection's central claim and audits it: ten probes at consecutive prime gaps, six genuinely independent and four provably the same quantity in other notation, with no residual anywhere.

**The two most useful papers here** are arguably not results at all:

- [The Null-Model Discipline](Null_Model_Discipline.md) — five ways to fool yourself, each caught in the act here. The most transferable material in the collection; none of it is specific to primes.
- [The Prime Prediction Budget](Prime_Prediction_Budget.md) — how far structure gets you toward the next prime, and where the wall is.

**The best concrete result** is the mod-6 traffic rule in [The Switchback Law](Switchback_Law.md) §2: two consecutive gaps can never both be ≡2 nor both ≡4 (mod 6). A prohibition, not a tendency.
