## One Wheel, Many Shadows — the audit

**The claim.** Every statistic anyone in this collection has pointed at consecutive prime gaps is accounted for by the primorial wheel (equivalently the Hardy–Littlewood singular series), with no residual detectable against a matched null.

**The caveat that makes the claim honest, and it is the important part.** These are *not* ten independent confirmations. Several are the same quantity in different clothes — provably so, by algebra. Counting them as independent would inflate one result into ten. The column below sorts them.

| probe | independent, or a restatement? | verdict vs the wheel |
|---|---|---|
| Jumping champions $6, 30, 210$ | **independent** — gap frequency distribution | wheel (classical, ORW 1999) |
| Forbidden widths $\{2,4,6,10\}$ | **independent** — combinatorial, not statistical | wheel, and *proven* not merely measured |
| Seven Sisters ($2p+k$) | **independent** — different construction, doubled scale | wheel |
| Offset-correlation comb $C(g)$ | **independent** — pair correlation at fixed offset | *is* the singular series, to ~1% |
| Prime structure factor | **independent** — frequency domain, Fourier dual | wheel as Bragg peaks |
| Consecutive-gap anti-correlation $-0.05$ | **independent** — the lag-1 statistic itself | wheel, 100%, plateau at $Q\approx300$ |
| Windowed gap memory $+1.97$ pp | **partly** — same gap sequence, longer window | wheel, ~99% from $Q=100$ |
| Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan mod-6 bias | **partly** — residue face of the same correlations | wheel, reproduced to 0.1 pp |
| Prime-triangle $45°$ angle | **restatement** — $\alpha$ *is* $g/p$ wearing trigonometry | wheel, trivially (same object) |
| Balance ratio $K\to1/3$ | **restatement** — reduces to $\mathrm{cov}(g_n,g_{n+1})$ by definition | wheel, trivially (same object) |

**So the defensible form of the claim is:** six genuinely distinct probes — combinatorial, frequency-domain, fixed-offset, gap-frequency, doubled-scale, and lag-1 — all land on the wheel with no residual; and four further statistics that *look* independent are provably the same objects. **Both halves are the result.** The second half is not a weakness; showing that apparently unrelated statistics are algebraically identical is the collection's unification thesis doing its job.

**What is not claimed.** That the wheel explains the primes. It explains the *gap statistics anyone here has measured*. The escape — which open slot is actually prime — remains the parity barrier, irreducible at $\approx0.26$ bits, and nothing here touches it.

### The balance ratio (2026-09)

- **[fact] Exact gap identity.** For any $a<b<c$ with gaps $g_1,g_2$ and mean $m$: $K-\tfrac13 = \tfrac{2}{27}\,(g_1^2+g_1g_2+g_2^2)/m^2$, where $K=(a^2+b^2+c^2)/(a+b+c)^2$ is the quantity carrying Koide's lepton relation. Verified as exact rational arithmetic. → [Balance Ratio §2](2_One_Wheel_Many_Shadows/PG_Balance_Ratio_And_Koide.md)
- **[fact] The hexagonal form's 3-divisibility *is* the mod-6 repeat.** With half-gaps $h=g/2$, $3\mid(h_1^2+h_1h_2+h_2^2) \iff g_1\equiv g_2 \pmod 6$; and for consecutive primes the patterns $(4,4)$ and $(2,2)$ are impossible, so this forces both $\equiv0$. **Hence the Eisenstein norm of the half-gap pair is divisible by 3 exactly when three consecutive primes share a residue mod 6** — a doubled Lemke Oliver–Soundararajan event. Measured $17.408\%$ against $18.492\%$ for independent transitions and $12.500\%$ for even odds; wheel surrogates reproduce it from $Q=30$ up. → [Balance Ratio §2.1](2_One_Wheel_Many_Shadows/PG_Balance_Ratio_And_Koide.md)
- **[mirage] The balance ratio is not a new prime signal.** Its entire deviation from a gap-shuffled null equals $\mathrm{cov}(g_n,g_{n+1})$ **by the definition of covariance** — a reduction, not a prediction, true of any sequence including random data. Measured $-0.708\%$ against $-0.719\%$ implied. No residue class mod 6 or mod 30 carries an excess beyond $1.3\sigma$, while the same test detects the LOS bias immediately and finds the wheel reproduces it to $0.1$ pp. → [Balance Ratio §5–§6](2_One_Wheel_Many_Shadows/PG_Balance_Ratio_And_Koide.md)
- **[fact] No prime–lepton connection.** Koide's $2/3$ is exactly the $45°$ condition and the midpoint of $K$'s range $[1/3,1]$; the primes run to the opposite corner ($\theta\to0$) while the leptons sit at $45°$. The shared $45°$ is shared coordinates. The two *prime* angles, however, do share a driver: both are linear in $g/p$. → [Balance Ratio §3, §7](2_One_Wheel_Many_Shadows/PG_Balance_Ratio_And_Koide.md)

### Null-model discipline (methodological, and the most transferable material here)

- **[fact] Differencing manufactures structure.** First differences of *any* i.i.d. sequence have lag-1 autocorrelation exactly $-\tfrac12$. Shuffle the *gaps* and then difference; never shuffle the differences.
- **[fact] Which layer you shuffle decides what you test.** Gap-shuffle tests "is it the wheel"; change-shuffle tests "is it the monotonic skeleton."
- **[fact] A shuffle null is only valid where the shuffle preserves every hard constraint the statistic conditions on.** Sorting a gap statistic by residue class and comparing against shuffled gaps is meaningless — a gap of 4 cannot follow $p\equiv5\pmod 6$, so the null's triples are arithmetically impossible. Go generative instead.
- **[fact] A generative null must stay strictly weaker than what it models.** "Coprime to all primes up to $\sqrt x$" is not weaker — it is a definition of primality. Report the **pool ratio** (candidates ÷ $\pi(x)$) beside every surrogate result and sweep $Q$; below $\approx1.25$ the comparison is circular.
- **[fact] Show the instrument has power before believing a null result.** Confirm the test still detects something known — LOS in the counts, or a deliberately too-shallow wheel.

→ all five in [Differencing Trap §2, §5.1, §8](2_One_Wheel_Many_Shadows/Prime_Gap_Memory_Differencing_Trap.md)

## Twin Bertrand

- **[conj] Twin-Prime Bertrand Postulate (TPB):** $T_{k+1}<2T_k$ for every twin $T_k\ge11$. Weaker than the twin-prime conjecture, stronger than bounded gaps; immediate under Hardy–Littlewood, open unconditionally.
- **[emp] Angle-record theorem — ✅ independently re-verified 2026-09-20.** Every closest-to-$45°$ angle record of the Prime Triangle (with $p_n\ge3$) is a twin prime — a geometric restatement of TPB, and by Thm 6 *logically equivalent* to it, so this is the load-bearing claim of the series. Checked with exact integer arithmetic over every consecutive prime pair to $10^8$: **440,312 records, 440,312 twins, zero non-twin records.** The correspondence is not merely inclusion — **the record sequence *is* the twin sequence.** That converse is now proved as **Proposition 6A** and is *unconditional*: every twin beats every earlier pair, by Lemma 4 with $P<T<2T$. So the clean statement is an asymmetry — **the record sequence always contains every twin, and TPB is exactly the claim that it contains nothing else**, giving the usable form *TPB fails ⟺ some angle-record is not a twin*. Holds under both readings of the record definition; the only wrinkle is that $(3,5)$ drops out if the pair $(2,3)$ is allowed to set the opening record, since $2/3 > 3/5$. → [PG II](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/PG_II_AngleRecord.md), [`check_angle_records.py`](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/scripts/check_angle_records.py)
- **[conj] Generalized Bertrand Principle (GBP):** TPB extends to cousins $(p,p+4)$ and sexy primes $(p,p+6)$, all verified to $10^{10}$; uniform envelope $G<0.171(\log P)^{3.22}$. → [PG III](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/PG_III_GBP.md)
- **[thm] Prime-Square-Difference identity:** $C_2^2-C_1^2=p_{n+2}^2-p_n^2$ — confirmed, and in fact needs no primality (the shared $p_{n+1}$ terms cancel for any three numbers). $\mathrm{PSD}_n=(p_{n+2}^2-p_n^2)/12$ is an integer for $p_n\ge5$, threshold sharp. **⚠️ The last-digit claim (0/4/6) is published for $p_n\ge5$ and is false there** — $(5,7,11)$ gives $\mathrm{PSD}=8$, the sole counterexample below $5\times10^6$; the proof's mod-5 step has nothing to stand on at $p=5$ itself. **True for $p_n\ge7$.** Further: the digits are not equidistributed — $0$ at $48.97\%$ against $25.51\%$ each for $4$ and $6$, with $4$ and $6$ *exactly* equal. → [PG I](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/PG_I_PrimeTriangle.md), [`check_psd.py`](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/scripts/check_psd.py)
- **[mirage] Twin-gap exponent — ⚠️ not reproduced (2026-09-20).** Published as $G_k\sim0.70(\log T_k)^{1.866}$, *"below the Hardy–Littlewood $\beta=2$."* Measured over four decades to $10^8$: mean $G \sim 0.792(\log T)^{1.978}$ — **the exponent is essentially 2, the HL value**, against the prediction $(\log T)^2/C_2 = 0.757(\log T)^2$. The claimed shortfall below 2 looks like the usual under-estimate from a log-log fit over a short range. → [`check_envelope_fits.py`](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/scripts/check_envelope_fits.py)
- **[mirage] The GBP envelope $G<0.171(\log P)^{3.22}$ — ⚠️ violated (2026-09-20).** 204 violations below $10^8$, worst $1.87\times$ at $T=850{,}349$ ($G=1452$ against a bound of $775$). The fit was performed on $\sup(G/T)$, which is attained at *small* $T$, then stated as a bound on $G$, whose extreme sits at *large* $T$ — different extremals, and the transfer fails. The exponent is not pinned either: anything in $[2.5,4.0]$ is arguable, and $R^2=0.982$ is an artifact of fitting two parameters to five *nested* running suprema. **What survives is the shape** — extreme twin gaps grow faster than the typical $(\log T)^2$, alike across constellations. → [envelope fits](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/results/uniform_envelope_fits.md)
- **[emp] Δx cascade:** doubled twins $2p_k$ on a prime-rank axis; the step $\Delta x_k$ counts primes in $(2p_k,2p_{k+1}]$ and is a Poisson mixture, macro-linear ($R^2\approx0.996$) with chance-level micro-collinearity. → [FS–TB Δx Analysis](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/FS_TB_DeltaX_Analysis.md)
- **[emp/mirage] The Twin-Slope Ceiling, and the "Filling Condition".** On the Factor Skyline consecutive primes form a segment whose slope is bounded by $\arctan(2/3)\approx33.69°$, attained **iff** the pair is a twin — **verified over every consecutive-prime segment to $10^6$, zero exceptions** (though the published proof's general case is broken and is repaired in place). The paper then names a *Filling Condition* as "the only obstacle" to the twin prime conjecture and claims it is strictly weaker than the parity barrier. **⚠️ Both claims fail.** Its Form 3 is verbatim PG II's TPB, so it is the collection's own conjecture rediscovered, not a new obstacle; TPB implies the twin prime conjecture unaided, so the FS structure does no work; and the parity argument misreads the barrier, which blocks the *existential* statement too. **Reframed 2026-09-20 around what survives:** a *second, independent geometric route to TPB*. The prime triangle reaches it via an angle rising to $45°$; the skyline via a slope capped at $\arctan(2/3)$, attained iff twin. Different coordinates, different extremal quantities, same inequality — which is evidence the conjecture is **natural**, not that it is true. Cross-referenced both ways. → [Twin-Slope Ceiling](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/FS_twin_prime_geometry.md), [PG II](3_Twin_Bertrand_Prime_Geometry/PG_II_AngleRecord.md)
